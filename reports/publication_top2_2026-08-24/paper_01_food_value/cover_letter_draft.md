# Cover Letter Draft — Paper 1

Dear Editors of *Artificial Life*,

Please consider the manuscript “Experience-Based Food Valuation in an Unlabeled
Artificial Ecology” as an Article. The manuscript studies a narrow question at
the intersection of artificial life, embodied adaptation, and ecological
decision-making: whether an agent can change its diet from the realized energetic
consequences of eating without receiving semantic food labels or an externally
specified task score.

Across ten paired random seeds, enabling type-level memory of realized food
energy reduced consumption of the lower-value food in every pair. A matched
mechanism ablation removed the skipping behavior, and agent-level traces place
experience with the higher-value comparator before the behavioral change. The
manuscript deliberately limits the conclusion to individual, within-lifetime
adaptation in the tested abundant-energy regime. It does not claim semantic
understanding, social transmission, fitness improvement, or open-ended evolution.

We believe the work fits *Artificial Life* because it examines how adaptive,
life-like behavior can arise from coupling an embodied agent to energetic
consequences in a synthetic ecology, while using explicit controls to distinguish
learned behavior from encounter and implementation artifacts.

This manuscript is substantially distinct from the planned companion manuscript
on time-varying toxicity. The present submission concerns whether stationary
outcome-grounded food valuation works. The companion concerns a representation-
level failure when a single observable food type hides multiple temporal safety
states; none of its headline results are used as primary evidence here.
