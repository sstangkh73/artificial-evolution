# Cover Letter - Paper 2

Dear Editors of *Artificial Life*,

Please consider “When Intermittent Safety Lures the Learner: Temporal Toxicity
Under Perceptual Aliasing” as a Letter. The manuscript reports a concise negative
and counterintuitive result from an artificial-life food-learning system. A
learner that correctly avoids a constant poison can instead pursue a food that is
toxic only in a hidden temporal state, because intermittent safety raises the
type-level average above a consistently safe alternative.

The representation-level inability to discriminate food age is analytic. The
lure magnitude is evaluated across 30 seeds, 100 agents per seed, a parameter
sweep, and four illustrative learning rules. The manuscript separates those two
claims and explicitly states that no survival or evolutionary fitness cost has
yet been demonstrated in the controlled sated regime.

We believe the result fits *Artificial Life* because it identifies a failure mode
that arises from the coupling of an outcome-grounded learner with hidden physical
state in a synthetic ecology. The novelty claim is the inversion from avoidance
to pursuit and its analytic boundary, not the general existence of partial
observability.

This Letter is distinct from a planned companion food-value manuscript. The companion
asks whether stationary value learning works and uses paired full-run meal counts.
This Letter asks why that representation fails under hidden time-varying state
and uses separate controlled toxin datasets. It does not reuse the companion's
primary result as a contribution.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Reproduction code and
source-data tables are included as supplementary material with the submission.
