Please consider our Article, **“When Does a Better Representation Pay? Survival Effects of Resource-Age Information in Artificial Foraging Under Temporal Toxicity,”** for publication in *Artificial Life*.

The article fits the journal's scope because it uses a synthesized, spatial artificial ecology to study a basic property of life-like adaptive behavior: how an embodied forager's internal representation interacts with resource dynamics, energetic constraints, and survival. The study asks whether giving a learner access to resource age resolves a food-choice problem in which one food category changes from toxic to safe and back to toxic, and whether the resulting behavioral improvement benefits survival.

The main contribution is a controlled separation between representational sufficiency and ecological benefit. A type-by-age value key restored safe-versus-toxic discrimination, whereas a capacity-matched sham key did not. A preregistered 30-seed experiment then varied risky-fruit energy across five realized levels. The registered slope was positive, but the level-specific results showed survival costs at four levels and no detected cost at the highest level, with sensitivity to that endpoint. A separate preregistered 50-seed replication found shorter follow-up under age-varying toxicity than under two constant-potency controls. We report these boundaries directly rather than presenting the representation as uniformly beneficial.
