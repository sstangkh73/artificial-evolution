Please consider our Article, **“Correct State Information Is Not Enough: Discrimination and Survival Diverge in a Toxic-Safe-Toxic Artificial Ecology,”** for publication in *Artificial Life*.

The article fits the journal's scope because it uses a synthesized, spatial artificial ecology to study a basic property of life-like adaptive behavior: how an embodied forager's internal representation interacts with resource dynamics, energetic constraints, and survival. The study asks whether giving a learner access to resource age resolves a food-choice problem in which one food category changes from toxic to safe and back to toxic, and whether improved discrimination necessarily improves survival under a fixed learning and decision policy.

The main contribution is a controlled separation between representational sufficiency and ecological outcome. A type-by-age value key restored safe-versus-toxic discrimination, whereas type-only and capacity-matched sham keys showed a small systematic age-blind offset whose source could not be identified from archived telemetry. In the internally frozen 30-seed E6 experiment, however, the age key produced shorter founder follow-up than the equal-capacity sham at fruit energies 4, 5, 6, and 8, and no detected advantage at 10. The registered age-key-minus-sham slope was positive but depended on the energy-10 endpoint and was not established to exceed the planned one-tick-per-energy effect scale in a post hoc minimum-effect sensitivity test. The endpoint also crossed the fixed policy's staple-rejection boundary and became senescence-dominated. A separate internally frozen 50-seed T2-RB replication under monotonic detoxification found shorter follow-up under age-varying toxicity than under two constant-payoff controls, despite lower realized toxin dose. We report the primary negative result before secondary, descriptive, multiplicity-sensitive, and endpoint-sensitive findings.
