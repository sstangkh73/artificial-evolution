Dear Editors,

Please consider our Article, **“When Does a Better Representation Pay? Survival Effects of Resource-Age Information in Artificial Foraging Under Temporal Toxicity,”** for publication in *Artificial Life*.

The article fits the journal's scope because it uses a synthesized, spatial artificial ecology to study a basic property of life-like adaptive behavior: how an embodied forager's internal representation interacts with resource dynamics, energetic constraints, and survival. The study asks whether giving a learner access to resource age resolves a food-choice problem in which one food category changes from toxic to safe and back to toxic, and whether the resulting behavioral improvement benefits survival.

The main contribution is a controlled separation between representational sufficiency and ecological benefit. A type-by-age value key restored safe-versus-toxic discrimination, whereas type-only and capacity-matched sham keys did not. In a preregistered 30-seed experiment under a toxic-safe-toxic profile, the age key improved founder follow-up relative to the type-only learner at fruit energies 4 and 5. The sham produced still longer follow-up through energy 8, however. The registered age-key-minus-sham slope was positive but depended on the energy-10 endpoint. A separate preregistered 50-seed replication under monotonic detoxification found shorter follow-up than two constant-potency controls within the same high-drain configuration. We report the registered, secondary, descriptive, and endpoint-sensitive results separately.
