# YSC Competition Strategy

Prepared: `2026-06-21`

## 1. Main objective

The `YSC` objective is not to present every subsystem in the repository.

The objective is to convince judges of four things:

1. the project is original
2. the project is scientifically real
3. the student understands the system deeply
4. the work has a credible path to become even more important at `ISEF`

## 2. What to emphasize most

### Emphasize

- the project asks a real scientific question, not just a programming question
- the system has embodied constraints: energy, ecology, reproduction, trade-offs
- the project already produced measurable discoveries
- the project includes falsification and negative-result analysis
- the next stage is clearly defined

### De-emphasize

- giant lists of features
- speculative far-future civilization behaviors
- every mode in the CLI
- every artifact directory in `data/`

Too much breadth can make the project look unfocused.

## 3. Best three-result structure for YSC

Recommended result stack:

### Result 1: ecology works

Message:
the simulated world produces a usable ecological loop rather than random noise.

### Result 2: agents learn something real

Message:
agents return to rewarding plant-food locations much more than chance predicts.

### Result 3: the transition barrier is real

Message:
stable multi-generation evolution does not automatically appear; the system exposed specific structural blockers.

This three-step sequence is clean and persuasive.

## 4. Best judge-facing story arc

Recommended progression:

1. Problem
   - most artificial-life systems either over-script intelligence or under-model ecology
2. Research question
   - what conditions let adaptive behavior become stable evolution?
3. Method
   - build a resource-limited world with bodies, traits, reproduction, and ecological feedback
4. Positive findings
   - ecological loop, return-to-reward, seed causality
5. Critical discovery
   - stable evolution is blocked by hidden structural gates
6. Impact
   - the work contributes both a platform and a barrier analysis

## 5. Judge psychology: what helps

Judges tend to respond well when a student can do these things:

- define exactly what a result means
- explain what it does not mean
- describe the control or comparison baseline
- admit when a claim got narrowed after falsification
- show what the next experiment must prove

This project can do all of those if prepared well.

## 6. Judge psychology: what hurts

These are the biggest presentation risks:

- sounding bigger than the evidence
- drowning the board in terminology
- making the project sound like a personal dream instead of a tested study
- confusing “interesting output” with “supported conclusion”

## 7. How to answer the hidden question

At `YSC`, judges are often asking a hidden question:

`Is this student doing real science, or just making a complex simulation?`

The answer must be:

- the project uses hypotheses
- the project uses controls and comparison logic
- the project logs failures
- the project updates its claims after falsification

That is what separates research from a software demo.

## 8. Presentation priority order

Use this order when time is limited:

1. research question
2. why this matters
3. world design and variables
4. strongest result 1
5. strongest result 2
6. strongest negative-result insight
7. next milestone toward `ISEF`

## 9. Minimum YSC-ready package

The project is `YSC-ready` when it has:

- one clear thesis statement
- one board or slide deck that follows a single narrative
- 2 to 3 validated headline results
- one explicit limitations section
- one explicit next-step plan to reach `ISEF`

## 10. Recommended verdict

Current strategic verdict:

`The project should be optimized to win YSC through rigor and originality, not through overclaiming scale.`
