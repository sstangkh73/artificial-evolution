# Judge Questions and Answer Directions

Prepared: `2026-06-21`

These are not polished scripts.
They are recommended answer directions that stay aligned with the current evidence.

## 1. What is your main research question?

Recommended direction:

`I am studying what conditions are necessary for artificial agents in a resource-limited world to move from short-term survival behavior into stable multi-generation evolution.`

## 2. What is novel about your work?

Recommended direction:

- the project combines ecology, body trade-offs, family/reproduction constraints, and experimental telemetry in one student-built platform
- the work is not only about getting nice behavior, but about identifying what structurally blocks the transition to stable evolution

## 3. Did the agents really learn, or are they just following rules you gave them?

Recommended direction:

- I did not encode farming knowledge or semantic understanding
- I measured behaviors such as return-to-reward location and seed-causality outcomes
- my claim is narrow: early adaptive behavior and ecological feedback, not human-like understanding

## 4. Do you already have true evolution in the system?

Recommended direction:

- not yet in the strongest sense I want
- the code has inheritance and mutation components, but the scientific bottleneck is demonstrating stable multi-generation dynamics under valid replication
- identifying that barrier is one of the main findings of the current stage

## 5. Why is a negative result still important?

Recommended direction:

- because it reveals where the transition fails
- in this project, failure was informative: it exposed hard reproduction gates, population instability, and replication issues that would otherwise stay hidden
- that makes the next stage much more scientifically grounded

## 6. How do you know your results are reliable?

Recommended direction:

- I use explicit metrics and comparison baselines
- I record structured outputs and reports
- I also identified places where reliability was not yet strong enough, such as seed independence in some runs
- I treat those as methodological issues to fix, not facts to ignore

## 7. What are the biggest limitations right now?

Recommended direction:

- stable mortal population dynamics are not yet solved
- some claims must stay narrow until replication is fully audited
- the current strongest results are about early learning and ecological coupling, not full open-ended evolution

## 8. If you had more time, what is the next decisive experiment?

Recommended direction:

- I would run a controlled transition study on stable reproduction and endogenous mutation
- specifically, I would compare regimes with and without home clustering, stochastic reproduction, mortality desynchronization, and mutation separation

## 9. Why should this project go from YSC to ISEF?

Recommended direction:

- because the platform is already deep enough to support a serious next-stage result
- the current work established the substrate and the real bottlenecks
- the next stage can directly test a globally interesting artificial-life question with much stronger rigor

## 10. What is the one thing you want judges to remember?

Recommended direction:

`This project does not just simulate life-like behavior; it experimentally studies what prevents artificial worlds from crossing into robust evolution, and that makes both the positive and negative results scientifically valuable.`
